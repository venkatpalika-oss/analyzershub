Yokogawa ZR22G

Zirconia in-situ oxygen analyzer for combustion control in boilers, furnaces, heaters, and process kilns.

Alarm Decision Trees → Back to Analyzer Search →

Working Principle

In the documented ZR22G detector / ZR402G converter system, the zirconia cell operates at 750 °C. Its voltage depends on the logarithm of the oxygen partial-pressure ratio between reference air and sample gas (the Nernst relationship). The converter calculates oxygen concentration from this voltage.

Main Components

  • Zirconia sensor cell
  • Heater assembly
  • Reference air system
  • In-situ probe assembly
  • ZR transmitter / converter
  • Signal and heater cabling

Calibration — ZR22G / ZR402G

The manual describes a low-oxygen “zero” gas (typically about 1% O₂ in nitrogen) and an air “span” gas (about 21% O₂). Enter the actual gas concentrations and follow the applicable flow, stabilization and calibration sequence in chapter 9.

Reference air serves the reference side of the cell; calibration gas is introduced to the measurement side. They have different functions. Nitrogen alone is not the generic zero-gas instruction for this system.

These notes apply to the cited ZR22G / ZR402G configuration, not every zirconia analyzer.

Major Troubleshooting Areas

  • High drift: sensor aging, contamination, unstable reference air
  • Calibration failure: incorrect gas, leaks, poor flow
  • Heater alarms: heater burnout, wiring faults
  • Cell voltage: interpret with measured oxygen, temperature and the chapter 12 diagnostics; voltage alone does not establish a failed cell.
  • Signal noise: grounding or shielding issues
